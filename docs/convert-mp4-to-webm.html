How to convert MP4 to WebM without uploading your video

MP4 plays everywhere, but WebM files are smaller and built for the web — the right format when a video lives on a page rather than in a player. The conversion takes one command, and your video never has to leave your computer.

November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

First: this one needs a re-encode — there is no copy shortcut

MP4 and WebM are containers, and the video inside them is different: an MP4 usually holds H.264 video, and H.264 is not valid inside a WebM file. So unlike converting WebM to MP4 — where the streams can just be copied into a new box — going the other way means re-encoding the video to VP9 and the audio to Opus, the codecs WebM is built around. Re-encoding takes longer than a copy and loses a little quality, so pick a quality setting you're happy with and keep the original MP4.

Method 1: FFmpeg (fastest, one command)

FFmpeg is the free, open-source tool that does the actual converting inside most converter apps. Install it once from ffmpeg.org (or brew install ffmpeg on a Mac, sudo apt install ffmpeg on Ubuntu/Debian), then run:

ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -c:v libvpx-vp9 -crf 30 -b:v 0 -c:a libopus output.webm

-crf 30 is the quality setting for VP9 — lower is better quality and a bigger file; 25 is near-transparent, 30 is a good balance, 35 is visibly smaller. -b:v 0 puts VP9 in constant-quality mode so the CRF value actually controls the result. VP9 encodes slower than H.264 — that's the price of the smaller file — so for a quick draft, trade a little quality for speed:

ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -c:v libvpx-vp9 -crf 35 -b:v 0 -deadline good -cpu-used 2 -c:a libopus output.webm

Method 2: VLC (no command line)

If you'd rather click than type, VLC can do the same job:

  1. Open VLC, go to Media → Convert / Save.
  2. Click Add, pick your MP4 file, then Convert / Save.
  3. Under Profile, choose Video – VP8 + Vorbis (WebM).
  4. Click Browse, choose where to save the .webm, and press Start.

VLC's WebM profile uses VP8, the older sibling of VP9 — the file plays everywhere WebM plays, but it's a little bigger than a VP9 encode at the same quality. For the smallest file, use the FFmpeg command above.

What to avoid

  • Online converters. They upload your video to someone else's server, convert it there, and send it back. That's slow for big files, and your video — possibly a private one — sits on a server you don't control. For anything you wouldn't post publicly, convert locally.
  • Free converter apps full of ads. When we sampled the newest 1-star reviews of the top video converters on Google Play, the same complaints kept coming up: ads interrupting the conversion, formats locked behind a paywall, and outputs that come out corrupt or unplayable. If an app makes you watch an ad to convert a file, it's selling your attention, not converting your video.

Rule of thumb: WebM is for the web — smaller files, HTML5 video, VP9. MP4 is for everything else — older phones, editors, and anywhere compatibility matters more than size. If the file has to play on an older phone or in an editor, keep it MP4.
