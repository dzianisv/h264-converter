How to convert video to MP4 without uploading it

MP4 plays almost everywhere — phones, TVs, browsers, editors. Whatever container your video is in now (MKV, MOV, WebM, AVI), the fix takes seconds, and your video never has to leave your computer.

November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

First: check what's inside your file

MP4 is a container — a box that holds video and audio streams. If the video inside your file is already H.264 and the audio is already AAC, MP4 accepts them as-is, and the conversion is just a re-boxing that takes seconds. If the streams are something else (VP9, HEVC, Opus), they have to be re-encoded, which takes longer. Check first, so you know which one you're doing:

ffprobe -v error -show_entries stream=codec_name,codec_type -of csv=p=0 input.mkv

If it prints h264,video (and aac,audio if the file has sound), use the copy below. Anything else — re-encode.

Method 1: FFmpeg (fastest, one command)

FFmpeg is the free, open-source tool that does the actual converting inside most converter apps. Install it once from ffmpeg.org (or brew install ffmpeg on a Mac, sudo apt install ffmpeg on Ubuntu/Debian), then run the command that matches your file.

If your file is already H.264 + AAC, copy the streams — no re-encoding, no quality loss, seconds even for a large file:

ffmpeg -i input.mkv -c copy output.mp4

-c copy means "copy every stream, don't re-encode". This works from MKV, MOV, or any container holding H.264 video and AAC audio.

If the copy fails, or your file holds VP9, HEVC, or another codec MP4 doesn't take, re-encode to H.264 video and AAC audio:

ffmpeg -i input.webm -c:v libx264 -crf 20 -c:a aac output.mp4

-crf 20 is a good quality setting for H.264 — lower is better quality and a bigger file; 18–23 is the sensible range for most videos. The same command works whatever the input is: MKV, MOV, WebM, AVI — just change the input name.

Method 2: VLC (no command line)

If you'd rather click than type, VLC can do the same job:

  1. Open VLC, go to Media → Convert / Save.
  2. Click Add, pick your video file, then Convert / Save.
  3. Under Profile, choose Video – H.264 + MP3 (MP4).
  4. Click Browse, choose where to save the .mp4, and press Start.

VLC re-encodes by default, so it's slower than the FFmpeg copy — but it works on any file, and there's nothing to install beyond VLC itself.

What to avoid

  • Online converters. They upload your video to someone else's server, convert it there, and send it back. That's slow for big files, and your video — possibly a private one — sits on a server you don't control. For anything you wouldn't post publicly, convert locally.
  • Free converter apps full of ads. When we sampled the newest 1-star reviews of the top video converters on Google Play, the same complaints kept coming up: ads interrupting the conversion, formats locked behind a paywall, and outputs that come out corrupt or unplayable. If an app makes you watch an ad to convert a file, it's selling your attention, not converting your video.

Rule of thumb: if the file plays fine and you only need a different container, copy the streams — never re-encode. Re-encoding is for when the codec itself has to change.
